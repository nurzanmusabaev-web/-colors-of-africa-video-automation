Why Africa's Deadliest Bird Never Uses Its Talons | Secretary Bird

Its foot is blunt, stubby, tipped with claws too short to grip anything. And it is about to kill a venomous snake with that foot alone — a kick that lands with roughly five times its own body weight, faster than the snake can react. This wildlife documentary follows one secretary bird from a single egg on a nest 2.5 metres wide to the moment it finally becomes the deadliest bird on the savanna, and answers the one question its body should not be able to answer: how does a bird that cannot grip, kill?

No narration. No music telling you how to feel. Just the days, in order.

📊 FACTS IN THIS FILM
• The only fully ground-hunting bird of prey; an adult walks 20-30 km a day on foot
• The kick strikes with roughly five times the bird's body weight, contact lasting 10-15 milliseconds — faster than the snake can strike back
• Legs are armoured in dense overlapping scales from foot to knee
• Incubation is about 45 days; clutches of 1-3 eggs hatch days apart, so chicks differ sharply in size
• Chicks cannot stand for weeks and move on their bare hock joints instead
• Usually only one chick per clutch survives to fledge
• Listed as Endangered by the IUCN since 2020
