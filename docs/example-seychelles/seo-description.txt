Seychelles Wildlife & Nature in the Most Beautiful Paradise on Earth

There are places on Earth that feel like whispers from another time. 115 islands scattered across the Indian Ocean, granite giants rising from water so clear it seems to hold light itself. This is the Seychelles — not just a beach, but one of the last places where the world remembers what it was before we changed it.

🌿 IN THIS FILM
• The granite islands themselves — remnants of the ancient supercontinent Gondwana, among the oldest rock on Earth
• The Coco de Mer, the largest seed in the plant kingdom, growing wild only in the Vallée de Mai on Praslin Island
• Aldabra giant tortoises — some alive today were already old when the American Civil War began
• The Seychelles black parrot and Seychelles magpie robin, two of the rarest birds on the planet
• Coral gardens, parrotfish, eagle rays, green sea turtles, and — if you're lucky — spinner dolphins
• How the Seychelles is fighting to keep this place alive: replanted reefs, banned fishing practices, and nearly half its land under protection

📊 FACTS IN THIS FILM
• 115 islands, isolated in the Indian Ocean, closer to open sky than to any continent
• The granite dates back roughly 400 million years — a fragment of Gondwana
• Underwater visibility reaches 30 metres on a good day
• A single Coco de Mer seed weighs up to 30 kg and takes seven years just to germinate
• About 80 plant species grow nowhere else on Earth, including the jellyfish tree, once thought extinct
• Aldabra giant tortoises can live 200 years and weigh over 250 kg; roughly 100,000 survive on Aldabra Atoll — the largest giant tortoise population on Earth
• Nearly half of the Seychelles' land area is protected, with marine reserves covering hundreds of square kilometres
